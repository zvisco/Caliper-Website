Measure angles and plan osteotomies on any X-ray

Caliper is a fast, private Mac app for orthopedic measurement. Open a radiograph, drop a few points, and read the angle. Then cut, hinge and see the correction.

Coming soon to the Mac App Store

What it does

3-point angles

Click three points and get the angle instantly. Drag any point to refine it under a magnifier.

Cobb angles

Measure the angle between two lines that never have to meet, for spinal curves and alignment.

Osteotomy simulation

Draw a cut, set the hinge and open or close the wedge. The image moves with the bone so you can see the result.

Several cuts at once

Stack multiple osteotomies, limit each to a window you outline, and share one window between cuts.

Reference lines

Draw straight lines for axes and joint lines, with an optional pixel length, in any colour.

Saved automatically

Every X-ray and its measurements are kept on your Mac. Reopen one later and carry on, with several open in tabs.

Image controls

Adjust brightness and contrast, invert the film, and rotate or flip it. Your measurements turn with the image.

Export

Save an annotated PNG for teaching, notes or a presentation, including fragments that hinge outside the frame.

Private by design

No account, no upload, no tracking. Images are never sent anywhere. Read the privacy policy.

Who it's for

Orthopedic surgeons, residents, fellows, medical students and physical therapists who want a quick way to measure and sketch a plan on a radiograph for teaching, discussion or study.

For reference and education only. Caliper is not a medical device and is not a certified diagnostic tool. Measurements are in degrees and image pixels and are not calibrated to real-world distance. Do not use it as the sole basis for diagnosis or treatment decisions.

Patient information and HIPAA. Caliper is not intended for storing protected health information. De-identify images before opening or sharing them. You are responsible for following HIPAA and your organization's policies.

Requirements

A Mac running macOS 13 or later, Apple silicon or Intel. Opens PNG, JPEG, and other common image formats; DICOM files need to be exported as images first.
